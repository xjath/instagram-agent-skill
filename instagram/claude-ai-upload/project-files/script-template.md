# Script framework (CONFIRMED 2026-11-23)

The layout every reel follows, so writing one is filling in blanks. Built from
the locked Providence script and the rules in `voice.md`. Still being shaped
with the user; open questions at the bottom.

Pace: 165 words a minute = about 2.75 words a second. Target 35-45s total
(about 100-120 words). Keep each line under ~4s (about 11 words), or plan a
shot change inside it.

## The skeleton (copy, then fill in the brackets)

Restaurant:
```
I spent $[TOTAL] at a [N] Michelin star restaurant in [CITY].
Come check it out with me.
This is [NAME], [PROOF LINE: stars, award or ranking; don't repeat the city].
[EXPERIENCE: first moment of the night], [your reaction].
Then [second moment].
The service? [one specific thing], [how it felt].
And the food? [one specific thing]. [reaction, or "don't get me wrong..." balance].
Bill? [menu price], [pairings], [extras], [tip].
Worth it?
[ANSWER]. [for a "no": "It wasn't for me."]
I give you the real experience behind every Michelin star and Key I visit, so follow for the next one.
```

Hotel:
```
I spent $[TOTAL STAY] at a [N] Michelin Key hotel in [CITY].
Come check it out with me.
This is [NAME]. [N] nights in a [ROOM TYPE]. [upgrade, if any]
[ROOM: the first thing you noticed], [your reaction].
[THE VIEW], [reaction]. [letdown here if it's the room]
The service? [one specific thing], [how it felt].
Then the amenities. [AMENITY 1], [reaction]. [AMENITY 2].
$[PER NIGHT] a night, [N] nights.
Worth it?
[ANSWER].
I give you the real experience behind every Michelin star and Key I visit, so follow for the next one.
```

## Restaurant layout

| # | Section | Time | Words | What goes here | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | HOOK | 0-3s | ~6-9 | "I spent $[TOTAL] at [NAME]." or "...at a [N] Michelin star restaurant." | NAME + ★ rating, price; up from frame 1 |
| 2 | CONTEXT | 3-8s | ~10-14 | City, plus one verified proof line (stars, award, ranking) | CITY / award |
| 3 | EXPERIENCE | 8-18s | ~20-28 | The night in order: room, ambiance, storytelling, extras. One specific moment, told as a reaction | 1-2 cards, 6 words max |
| 4 | SERVICE | 18-24s | ~12-16 | One specific + how it felt | "SERVICE: ..." |
| 5 | FOOD | 24-31s | ~14-20 | One specific + reaction. Letdown goes here if the food was the letdown, balanced ("don't get me wrong...") | "FOOD: ..." |
| 6 | THE BILL | 31-36s | ~12-16 | What the money covered: menu price, pairings, extras, tip | breakdown card |
| 7 | VERDICT | 36-39s | ~4-8 | "Worth it?" (pause) + answer. A "no" adds who it IS for | answer in red |
| 8 | SIGN-OFF | 39-43s | ~13 | "I give you the real experience behind every Michelin star and Key I visit, so follow for the next one." | FOLLOW FOR THE NEXT ONE |

## Flow: lead-ins between sections (applies to EVERY script, user 2026-11-23)

Every section hands off to the next with a lead-in, so the script reads like
one easy conversation. Never jump straight from one topic to the next.
Rotate the lead-ins so reels don't all sound the same.
The bank is a source of ideas, not required wording: on Le Cinq the user
preferred a version with lighter lead-ins ("And the dining room matches it",
"The service?", "And the food?") over one using the bank's phrases (2026-11-26).

Restaurants
| From -> to | Lead-ins to pick from |
|---|---|
| Hook -> context | "Okay, I need to tell you about this place." / "So let me tell you about it." |
| Context -> experience | "So first, ..." / "The night starts with ..." / "Right when we walk in, ..." |
| Experience moment -> next moment | "Then ..." / "And then out of nowhere, ..." / "And it gets better, ..." |
| Experience -> service | "And the whole night, the staff ..." / "And honestly, the people make it ..." |
| Service -> food | "So by the time the food comes out, the bar's already high." / "And then the food starts coming out." / "But you're really here for the food, right?" |
| Food highlight -> consistency or letdown | "And it never drops." / "But here's the thing, ..." / "Don't get me wrong, ..." |
| Food -> bill | "So what does a night like that cost?" / "Now, the bill." / "So how much was all of that?" |
| Bill -> verdict | "So, worth it?" / "Worth it?" |
| Verdict -> sign-off | (straight into) "I give you the real experience behind every Michelin star and Key I visit, so follow for the next one." |

Hotels
| From -> to | Lead-ins to pick from |
|---|---|
| Hook -> context | "Okay, let me show you this place." / "So here's what that gets you." |
| Context -> room | "The second you walk into the room, ..." / "First, the room." |
| Room -> view | "And then you look out the window." / "And the view?" |
| Room -> service | "And the staff, ..." / "And the whole stay, they ..." |
| Service -> amenities | "Then there's everything else." / "And when you leave the room, ..." |
| Amenities -> bill | "So what does a stay like that cost?" / "Now, the bill." |
| Bill -> verdict | "So, worth it?" |

Flow check before a script is shown:
- [ ] Read it top to bottom out loud (or in your head): does any line start a
      new topic with no lead-in? Add one.
- [ ] No two sections in a row start with the same word ("And", "So").
- [ ] Lead-ins come from the user's own voice (voice.md), never formal
      ("Moving on to the cuisine...").

## Rules every section follows
- The context line always starts "This is [NAME]..." (user, 2026-11-23),
  never "It's [NAME]".
- Tone (user, 2026-11-23): talk to the viewer like they're your best friend.
  "Okay, I need to tell you about this place", "you", "trust me",
  "I'm not kidding", "okay, but the...". Move through the night in order,
  with a reaction at each step. Not a list, and not a narrated story.
- Each section sets up the next one, starting from the venue intro (user,
  2026-11-23). E.g. the intro ends on a claim ("they know how to treat their
  guests like royalty") that the next section proves.
- Lead into each section, don't jump (user, 2026-11-23): e.g. service to
  food: "so by the time the food comes out, the bar's already high"; food
  to bill: "so what does a night like that cost?"
- React, don't label ("and you can feel it", not "the service was excellent").
- Contractions and "I / we / you".
- Every fact is from the user's notes or verified with a source.
- Each of the three talking points gets at least one spoken line. Gaps: ask.
- Letdowns are always mentioned if the notes have one; "none" can become
  "not one miss".


## Example: Providence (locked) mapped to the layout
1. HOOK: "$2,400 for dinner for two. Here's what you actually get." (made before the standard hook)
2. CONTEXT: "Providence, LA. One of the first LA spots ever to get three Michelin stars."
3. EXPERIENCE: wine cellar with the three-star Dom; the surprise private room
4. SERVICE: James Beard hospitality award; "you can really feel the care..."
5. FOOD: "Every dish hit."
6. THE BILL: $495 chef's tasting menu, pairings, cocktail, add-ons, tip
7. VERDICT: "Worth it? Definitely."
8. SIGN-OFF: (old) "Follow me, cuz you don't always get what you pay for."

## Decisions (user, 2026-11-23)
1. Order confirmed: Experience, Service, Food (restaurants); Room, Service,
   Amenities (hotels).
2. Hook price = the biggest true number, for shock value: the total bill for
   restaurants, the total stay for hotels. Per-person or per-night prices go
   in THE BILL section.
3. Providence will be redone on this framework, after the content (footage)
   is sorted.
