# Reel #11: Mizue, Taipei

Status: REOPENED 2026-11-27: version 2 was locked without an Experience line
(restaurant criteria: Food, Service, Experience). Version 3 DRAFT adds it.

## Facts used
- Mizue, Japanese omakase on the 2nd floor of Capella Taipei (Songshan
  District). Visited February 2026, for two; 0 stars at the visit.
- Entered the MICHELIN Guide Taiwan 2026 (unveiled 21 July 2026) straight at
  TWO stars (checked 2026-11-27 via search results: Focus Taiwan 2026-07-21,
  Taipei Times 2026-07-22, Commercial Times, Michelin's Taiwan 2026 release).
- CORRECTION: the backlog says "about a month later"; the guide came out about
  5 months after the February visit.
- Paid NT$21,000 for two = ~US$636 (~US$320pp) at TWD 0.0303; hook $600.
- Verdict: it was okay; would go back to see if the rice is fixed ("Not yet").
- Best: fish was really good; some of the most attentive, hospitable service
  in Taiwan. Letdown: the sushi rice needed work.
- Footage: mix of photos and videos of the food and presentation.
- Hook technique (backlog): 5 before & after (0 stars -> 2 stars).

## Hook decision (user, 2026-11-27)
Standard hook: "I spent $600 at a two Michelin star restaurant in Taipei."
User: keep it simple; viewers don't need to know it had 0 stars at the visit.
It is literally true now (Mizue holds two stars). Before & after angle dropped.

## Detail answers (user, 2026-11-27)
- Fish: sliced into thick pieces, so slightly more fish than rice. The soy
  sauce complemented the fish perfectly and gave it depth.
- Rice: a little too soft for the user's liking, and not enough vinegar. "When
  it comes down to sushi, the rice matters a lot. More than the fish."
- Service: they noticed the user is left-handed and moved the utensils to the
  left side whenever they replaced them.
- Verdict: not at the time the user went, but curious whether they improved
  the rice.

```
I spent $600 at a two Michelin star restaurant in Taipei.
Come check it out with me.
This is Mizue, inside the Capella hotel.
We sat at the sushi counter, so we got to watch the chef make every single piece right in front of us.
And the staff here pay attention to everything.
They noticed I'm left-handed, so every time they replaced my utensils, they moved them over to the left side.

And the rice was a little too soft for my liking, with not enough vinegar.
So, worth it?
```
