## Voice rules
- Loose, run-on, conversational. Chain thoughts with "so", "and", "because".
  Casual filler OK: "okay so", "honestly", "wanna", "gonna".
- Never clipped robotic one-liners in a row. React to food instead of labelling it.
- Never critic words: unctuous, mouthfeel, "a symphony of", elevated, curated.
- No emoji. Burned-in word-by-word captions, key word in red.
- Never invent facts; verify accolades before using them.

## Rating system (restaurants, updated by the user 2026-11-20)

No numbers. Every restaurant script talks through the same three points, in
words, then gives the verdict:

1. FOOD: taste, quality, how memorable it is. Drinks and pairings count here.
2. SERVICE: staff, hospitality, attentiveness.
3. EXPERIENCE: the room, ambiance, storytelling, extras.
VERDICT: worth it? (yes / no / not really). Price is weighed here.

Each point gets at least one spoken line, in the user's own words from their
notes. If the notes say nothing about a point, ask the user; never fill it in.
No numeric scores and no on-screen scorecard (an earlier out-of-10 version was
dropped the same day).

## Hook techniques (from an Instagram creator course the user took; saved 2026-11-22)

A checklist, not tested research. Every script notes which technique(s) its
hook uses. A strong hook lands in the first 1-3 seconds by creating
curiosity, showing value or triggering an emotion.

1. Establish authority: personal experience, achievements, media, research, experts.
2. Spark curiosity: intrigue that makes people watch to find the answer.
3. Highlight the benefits: what viewers will gain or learn.
4. Create relatability: familiar situations or experiences viewers share.
5. Before & after: a visible transformation or difference.
6. Challenge common beliefs: go against conventional wisdom.
7. Emphasize loss / FOMO: what viewers might miss out on.

Hooks so far (all "I paid $X... worth it?" pattern):
- #1 Providence: 2 curiosity.
- #2 Bulgari Tokyo: 1 authority + 2 curiosity.
- #3 Atomix: 1 authority + 6 challenge beliefs.
- #4 Banbo: 1 authority + 2 curiosity.
Not used yet: 3, 4, 5, 7. Vary the pattern so the series doesn't feel
predictable (Claude's judgment; check against Insights).

## Sign-off

## Giving a "no" without burning bridges (user, 2026-11-23)

Honest verdicts stay, but every "no" or "not really" script follows these:
- Criticise the experience, never the people (staff, chef, owners).
- Always say what was good too (the three talking points make this automatic).
  Atomix model: "the service was good... the food was good, but it felt flat."
- Every "no" verdict is worded "It wasn't for me." (STANDARD, user
  2026-11-23): personal taste, not a ruling on the restaurant. Add who it IS
  for only if the user asks.
- No mocking, sarcasm or pile-on language.
- Toronto restaurants: be extra careful, since the concierge access depends on
  front-of-house relationships there. Flag any Toronto "no" to the user first.
- Sponsored or comped content gets the same honest verdict, and any material
  connection (payment, free meals, discounts, trips) is disclosed visibly,
  above the "...more" cut (Canada's Competition Bureau; US FTC 16 CFR 255).
  General information, not legal advice.
