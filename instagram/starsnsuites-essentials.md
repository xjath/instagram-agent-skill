## Hook techniques (from an Instagram creator course the user took; saved 2026-11-22)

A checklist, not tested research. Every script notes which technique(s) its
hook uses. A strong hook lands in the first 1-3 seconds by creating
curiosity, showing value or triggering an emotion.

1. Establish authority: personal experience, achievements, media, research, experts.
2. Spark curiosity: intrigue that makes people watch to find the answer.
3. Highlight the benefits: what viewers will gain or learn.
4. Create relatability: familiar situations or experiences viewers share.
5. Before & after: a visible transformation or difference.
6. Challenge common beliefs: go against conventional wisdom.
7. Emphasize loss / FOMO: what viewers might miss out on.

- Every "no" verdict is worded "It wasn't for me." (STANDARD, user
  2026-11-23): personal taste, not a ruling on the restaurant. Add who it IS

  above the "...more" cut (Canada's Competition Bureau; US FTC 16 CFR 255).
  General information, not legal advice.

# Script framework (CONFIRMED 2026-11-23)

The layout every reel follows, so writing one is filling in blanks. Built from
the locked Providence script and the rules in `voice.md`. Still being shaped
with the user; open questions at the bottom.

Pace: 165 words a minute = about 2.75 words a second. Target 35-45s total
(about 100-120 words). Keep each line under ~4s (about 11 words), or plan a
shot change inside it.

## The skeleton (copy, then fill in the brackets)

Restaurant:
```
I spent $[TOTAL] at a [N] Michelin star restaurant in [CITY].
Come check it out with me.
This is [NAME], [PROOF LINE: stars, award or ranking; don't repeat the city].
[EXPERIENCE: first moment of the night], [your reaction].
Then [second moment].
The service? [one specific thing], [how it felt].
And the food? [one specific thing]. [reaction, or "don't get me wrong..." balance].
Bill? [menu price], [pairings], [extras], [tip].
Worth it?
[ANSWER]. [for a "no": "It wasn't for me."]
I give you the real experience behind every Michelin star and Key I visit, so follow for the next one.
```

Hotel:
```
I spent $[TOTAL STAY] at a [N] Michelin Key hotel in [CITY].
Come check it out with me.
This is [NAME]. [N] nights in a [ROOM TYPE]. [upgrade, if any]
[ROOM: the first thing you noticed], [your reaction].
[THE VIEW], [reaction]. [letdown here if it's the room]
The service? [one specific thing], [how it felt].
Then the amenities. [AMENITY 1], [reaction]. [AMENITY 2].
$[PER NIGHT] a night, [N] nights.
Worth it?
[ANSWER].
I give you the real experience behind every Michelin star and Key I visit, so follow for the next one.
```

## Flow: lead-ins between sections (applies to EVERY script, user 2026-11-23)

Every section hands off to the next with a lead-in, so the script reads like
one easy conversation. Never jump straight from one topic to the next.
Rotate the lead-ins so reels don't all sound the same.
The bank is a source of ideas, not required wording: on Le Cinq the user
preferred a version with lighter lead-ins ("And the dining room matches it",
"The service?", "And the food?") over one using the bank's phrases (2026-11-26).

Restaurants
| From -> to | Lead-ins to pick from |
|---|---|
| Hook -> context | "Okay, I need to tell you about this place." / "So let me tell you about it." |
| Context -> experience | "So first, ..." / "The night starts with ..." / "Right when we walk in, ..." |
| Experience moment -> next moment | "Then ..." / "And then out of nowhere, ..." / "And it gets better, ..." |
| Experience -> service | "And the whole night, the staff ..." / "And honestly, the people make it ..." |
| Service -> food | "So by the time the food comes out, the bar's already high." / "And then the food starts coming out." / "But you're really here for the food, right?" |
| Food highlight -> consistency or letdown | "And it never drops." / "But here's the thing, ..." / "Don't get me wrong, ..." |
| Food -> bill | "So what does a night like that cost?" / "Now, the bill." / "So how much was all of that?" |
| Bill -> verdict | "So, worth it?" / "Worth it?" |
| Verdict -> sign-off | (straight into) "I give you the real experience behind every Michelin star and Key I visit, so follow for the next one." |

Hotels
| From -> to | Lead-ins to pick from |
|---|---|
| Hook -> context | "Okay, let me show you this place." / "So here's what that gets you." |
| Context -> room | "The second you walk into the room, ..." / "First, the room." |
| Room -> view | "And then you look out the window." / "And the view?" |
| Room -> service | "And the staff, ..." / "And the whole stay, they ..." |
| Service -> amenities | "Then there's everything else." / "And when you leave the room, ..." |
| Amenities -> bill | "So what does a stay like that cost?" / "Now, the bill." |
| Bill -> verdict | "So, worth it?" |

Flow check before a script is shown:
- [ ] Read it top to bottom out loud (or in your head): does any line start a
      new topic with no lead-in? Add one.
- [ ] No two sections in a row start with the same word ("And", "So").
- [ ] Lead-ins come from the user's own voice (voice.md), never formal
      ("Moving on to the cuisine...").

## Rules every section follows
- The context line always starts "This is [NAME]..." (user, 2026-11-23),
  never "It's [NAME]".
- Tone (user, 2026-11-23): talk to the viewer like they're your best friend.
  "Okay, I need to tell you about this place", "you", "trust me",
  "I'm not kidding", "okay, but the...". Move through the night in order,
  with a reaction at each step. Not a list, and not a narrated story.
- Each section sets up the next one, starting from the venue intro (user,
  2026-11-23). E.g. the intro ends on a claim ("they know how to treat their
  guests like royalty") that the next section proves.
- Lead into each section, don't jump (user, 2026-11-23): e.g. service to
  food: "so by the time the food comes out, the bar's already high"; food
  to bill: "so what does a night like that cost?"
- React, don't label ("and you can feel it", not "the service was excellent").
- Contractions and "I / we / you".
- Every fact is from the user's notes or verified with a source.
- Each of the three talking points gets at least one spoken line. Gaps: ask.
- Letdowns are always mentioned if the notes have one; "none" can become
  "not one miss".

# FILE: detail-questions.md

# Detail questions: what to say in each script

Saved 2026-11-23. Coming up with what to say is the hardest part, so
instead of writing from a blank page, answer these quick questions for each
place. Answer in your own words (typed or dictated); Claude keeps your
wording and turns the answers into lines on the skeleton in
`script-template.md`.

The rule behind every question: a good line is ONE specific thing (a
moment, a dish, an object, something someone did) plus how it made you
feel. "The service was great" is a label; "they noticed I'm left-handed
and moved my cutlery" is a line.

## Restaurants

EXPERIENCE (the night, in order)
1. What happened right when you arrived, before any food?
2. What surprised you, or what didn't you expect?
3. Describe the room in a few words: lighting, noise, where you sat.
4. When did you first take your phone out to film? What was it?

SERVICE
5. Name one thing a staff member did that you remember.
6. Did anyone explain the dishes, a story, or the wine? How did it come across?
7. Did they notice anything without being asked (a refill, a birthday, a preference)?
8. If service was a letdown: what exactly happened?

FOOD (and drinks)
9. The one dish you'd order again. What was it, and what did it taste like or remind you of?
10. The weakest dish. What was wrong with it?
11. Drinks or pairings: one that stood out, good or bad?
12. How did you feel at the end: blown away, satisfied, bored, still hungry?

THE BILL
13. Exact total, and what it covered (menu, pairings, extras, tip, tax)?
14. Anything that surprised you on the bill?

VERDICT
15. Would you go back? Yes, no, or only for an occasion?
16. Who would you send here, and who should skip it?

## Hotels

ROOM
1. Your first reaction when you opened the door.
2. One design detail you noticed (materials, lighting, something unusual).
3. The view: what could you see, and when was it best (morning, night)?
4. Bed, bathroom, or anything in the room you'd want at home?
5. Anything wrong with the room?

SERVICE
6. Name one thing the staff did that you remember (check-in, concierge, room service).
7. Did they do anything without being asked?

AMENITIES
8. Which amenity did you use most, and what was it like?
9. Breakfast or the restaurant: one thing worth mentioning?
10. Any extra (car service, spa, welcome gift)?

THE BILL
11. Room type, nights, price per night, total, any upgrades (free or paid)?

VERDICT
12. Would you stay again, and who is this hotel for?

## Digging deeper (user, 2026-11-23)
If an answer is a label ("beautiful", "attentive", "very good") rather than
a specific thing, Claude asks follow-up questions before writing: what
exactly, what did it look/taste/feel like, what did someone do, what
surprised you. Ask until each talking point has one specific detail.

## How to answer
- Short is fine. Fragments are fine. "Lobster thing with the green sauce,
  tasted like the ocean, best bite of the night" is perfect.
- If you don't remember, say so; that part gets left out, never made up.
- Photos or videos help: they show what you remember and what can be filmed.

### What makes a script better (learned 2026-11-22)
- Lead with the payoff number and a promise ("$2,400 for dinner for two.
  Here's what you actually get.") rather than the same "I paid $X... worth
  it?" every time. Rotate the hook techniques.
- Contractions and "I / you / we" (the humanizer's biggest factor).
- Each talking point gets a reaction, not a label ("and you can feel it",
  "not one miss").
- End on a line that repeats a word from the hook, so the reel loops.
- Cut lines that repeat the verdict ("the entire experience was amazing").
- Keep each line under ~4s, or plan a shot change inside it.
